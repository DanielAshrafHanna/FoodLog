## Finding and decision

Dany identified excess empty space below Playlists and explicitly asked to reduce it while preserving stability. NAV-06/NAV-10 intentionally reserved space to stop filters and background actions moving the list. The previous implementation stacked a 44px filter-only rail above a separate 44px count/actions row. Its height was stable but too costly when there were no applied filters.

Replace those two rows with one **44px feedback strip**, keeping the existing Table Notes palette, typography, playlists, navigation, card media and access behavior:

- The visible/total count stays at the leading edge with a small minimum width. Its live announcement remains separate from the controls.
- Show all, Update order and category-labeled removable filters share the remaining native horizontal scroller, in DOM and keyboard order. Show all remains the first available recovery action; unavailable actions consume no horizontal space. The strip keeps its height regardless of the number of filters/actions.
- Long chip labels use bounded ellipsis; their full accessible removal names remain. The entire chip is the removal target. Touch targets remain at least 44px high, and focus rings sit inside the clipping boundary.
- A scoped focus handler reveals off-screen controls using only the rail’s horizontal scroll offset. Native focus alone left a chip partially clipped during keyboard testing. Do not use page-level scrollIntoView here.
- No expanding/collapsing height animation is added. The list starts 44px higher on phones (390px fixture: y405 → y361) and remains there during filtering. Desktop saves 16px because the existing playlist block still determines the shared row height.

This supersedes the earlier 88px reservation and full-width filter-only rail, not their stability rationale or functionality. The tradeoff is horizontal overflow when several filters/actions are present on a narrow phone. Retain direct removal, Show all’s playlist/sort-preserving reset, separate Bookmarks/status, explicit order acceptance, keyed reconciliation and existing URL/return semantics.

## Research and interpretation

[Google’s layout stability guidance](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-cls) recommends reserving space for dynamic content to avoid unexpected shifts. [NN/g’s filtering research](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/applying-filters/) emphasizes continuity and avoiding disruptive page-position changes during refinement. These principles support keeping a stable strip rather than collapsing and expanding a blank region. The particular 44px combined layout is our product-specific design inference, not a measured user preference or field CLS score.

The Impeccable Layout/Operate guidance informed the hierarchy: count as quiet metadata, actions as plain text, and removable filters as bounded chips. The incumbent identity remains authoritative.
